I started in Montpellier, in art school. Before I finished the BFA I had a public art commission along the city tramway, a Grand Prix from the LVMH concours, and a habit of building the technical side of other people's projects — live scores, sound installations, the first websites.

New York took the next twenty years. I was technical director at [Flux Factory]({{< relref "work/flux-factory" >}}), then ran the international residency program at [Location One]({{< relref "work/location-one" >}}), then co-founded [Residency Unlimited]({{< relref "work/residency-unlimited" >}}) and directed operations there for eight years: 450 artists, 250 public programs, half a million in annual revenue, and every website along the way. Arts non-profits are where you learn that the system, the budget, and the person using it are the same problem.

In 2019 I made the engineering formal at Flatiron School. Then five years as Manager of Web & Information Services at the Alliance for Young Artists & Writers, product owner of the Scholastic Awards portal — 300,000 applications, 80,000 regional awards, and 3,000 national awards a year, plus the modernization of the system underneath it with a team of design and engineering vendors.

I now work from Mexico City on product strategy and web development, mostly migrating small organizations off expensive hosted stacks onto static, CI/CD-driven, open-source ones. The last one cut licensing costs by ninety percent.

What has not changed across any of it: I like translating complexity into something a team can actually act on, and I like being useful to the mission, not just the ticket.
